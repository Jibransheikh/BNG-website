The National Environment Policy, 2026

This Policy replaces the National Environment Policy 2013, adopted as Sessional Paper No 10 of 2014. It provides the national policy framework for the protection, conservation and sustainable management of Kenya's environment and natural resources.

Why the Policy is being revised

The case for the revision of the Policy as stated in the National Environment Policy 2026 entails:

  • The 2013 policy was finalised before county governments became operational and before most of the environmental law now in force was enacted.
  • The courts have built a body of environmental jurisprudence on Articles 10, 42, 69 and 70 of the Constitution that the 2013 policy did not anticipate, on public participation, on the polluter pays principle, on intergenerational equity and on the standard of environmental assessment. Kenya has also assumed international obligations since 2013, among them the Paris Agreement and the Nationally Determined Contribution made under it, the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and the Agreement on marine biological diversity of areas beyond national jurisdiction.
  • The scientific basis has changed with the Sixth Assessment Report of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change and the assessments that followed it, and the 2013 policy provided no mechanism for taking new evidence into account.
  • Questions that did not arise in 2013 now require policy direction, including carbon markets, loss and damage, the just transition and the use of artificial intelligence in environmental decisions.

The Goal

The goal of this Policy is to provide a coherent, constitutionally grounded and evidence-based framework for the protection, conservation and sustainable management of Kenya's environment and natural resources, for the benefit of present and future generations.

Key Objectives

The Policy sets out 10 objectives to give effect to this goal. These include:

  • To give practical effect to the right to a clean and healthy environment
  • To settle environmental governance at both levels of government
  • To meet Kenya's obligations under the multilateral environmental agreements
  • To protect, conserve and restore terrestrial, freshwater, coastal and marine ecosystems and to reach and hold a tree cover of at least ten per cent
  • To prevent, control and abate pollution and to advance resource efficiency and a circular economy
  • To manage the environmental conditions on which climate resilience depends
  • To distribute environmental benefits and burdens fairly within and between generations
  • To mobilise finance for environmental management and to bring environmental cost into public finance and planning
  • To govern environmental data and the use of artificial intelligence
  • To monitor and evaluate implementation against measurable indicators

Guiding Principles

Twenty-two guiding principles inform how those objectives are pursued. They include sustainable development, the precautionary principle, polluter pays, intergenerational and intragenerational equity, public participation and environmental democracy, gender equality, disability mainstreaming, the best interests of the child, the rights of Indigenous Peoples and marginalised communities, subsidiarity and devolution, the ecosystem based approach, nexus governance, science and policy integration, the circular economy, adaptive governance and common but differentiated responsibilities.

Structure of the Policy

Key Chapters Of The National Environment Policy, 2026

The Policy opens with preliminary pages, works through the context in Chapters 1 to 7, sets out the substantive commitments in Chapters 8 to 14, and closes with two annexes. Choose a chapter below to expand and read what it covers.

Preliminary Pages

Foreword & Preface (To be inserted in the Second Draft), Abbreviations & Acronyms Executive Summary

Provides the context and overview of the National Environment Policy 2026, covering the political context and review process, contributors to its development, key abbreviations and acronyms, and a summary of the Policy's rationale, goal, objectives, guiding principles, commitments, implementation and next steps.

Chapter 1: Preamble To The National Environment Policy

Provides the context and rationale for the National Environment Policy 2026. It traces Kenya's environmental policy development since independence through key international and national milestones, including the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, following which Kenya initiated the National Environment Action Plan (NEAP) process, completed in 1994, the 1999 EMCA and the 2010 Constitution, and outlines the development of the 2013 Policy. It then sets out why the Policy requires revision, highlighting changes in legislation, constitutional jurisprudence, devolution, international commitments, scientific evidence, emerging environmental governance issues and policy development standards, before introducing the approach and structure of the National Environment Policy 2026.

Chapter 2: Policy Making & Implementation In A Devolved Government Context

Sets out the framework for environmental policy making and implementation within Kenya's devolved system of government. It explains the constitutional framework governing environmental responsibilities, the distribution of functions between the national and county governments, and the mechanisms for intergovernmental relations and coordination where functions are shared. It also identifies the gap in county environmental policy and establishes the basis for strengthening coordination, guidance and implementation of environmental governance at both levels of government.

Chapter 3: Rationale For The Revision Of Sessional Paper No. 10 Of 2014 On National Environment Policy

Sets out the rationale for revising the National Environment Policy 2013. It explains how Kenya's environmental governance landscape has changed over the past decade through developments in legislation, sectoral policies, international commitments, constitutional jurisprudence, scientific knowledge and devolution. It also highlights emerging environmental governance themes and the need to align the Policy with current policy formulation standards, providing the basis for a coherent, constitutionally grounded and forward-looking framework for environmental governance.

Chapter 4: Goal, Objectives & Guiding Principles

Sets out the goal, objectives and guiding principles of the National Environment Policy 2026, which provide the normative foundation for the policy directions that follow. It defines the Policy's overall goal of protecting, conserving and sustainably managing Kenya's environment and natural resources, sets out its 10 objectives and 22 guiding principles.

Chapter 5: Constitutional & Legal Foundation

Establishes the constitutional and legal foundation of the National Environment Policy 2026. It aligns the Policy with relevant constitutional provisions, particularly Articles 10, 42, 69 and 70, and incorporates key court decisions that have shaped environmental governance. It also sets standards for areas such as public participation, environmental justice, environmental assessments, liability, intergenerational equity and the protection of environmental rights.

Chapter 6: International Commitments & MEA Alignment

In a nutshell, Chapter 6 establishes how Kenya's international environmental commitments will be translated into national and county-level policy and action. It maps Kenya's obligations under key multilateral environmental agreements and frameworks, including climate change, biodiversity, chemicals and waste, marine environments, land degradation and disaster risk reduction, to the domestic policy responses required. It also sets out a domestication pathway linking each commitment to the relevant legal or policy instrument, accountable institution and reporting mechanism, while strengthening coordination between national and county governments.

Chapter 7: Overarching Policy Commitments

Chapter 7 brings together the overarching policy commitments that apply across the entire National Environment Policy 2026. It sets out the common commitments that guide all sectors, chapters and both levels of government, including constitutional compliance, implementation of international environmental obligations, ecosystem-based management, environmental quality and pollution control, climate and energy governance, cross-sectoral coordination, equity and public participation, devolved environmental governance, financing, and monitoring and evaluation. It provides a single view of what the Policy commits the Government to before the specific environmental governance areas are addressed in the subsequent chapters.

Part II

The chapters that follow set out the substantive commitments of this Policy. They begin with the natural resource base and with environmental quality and public health, turn to climate change, the energy transition and the emerging governance themes, and then to the cross-sectoral and cross-cutting arrangements. They conclude with the institutions, the financing and the monitoring and evaluation through which this Policy is delivered.

Each chapter is structured around a Chapter Policy Objective and Overview, a situational analysis that explains the issue, context, challenges and gaps, and policy statements that set out what the Government commits to do in response.

Top Line Comments

BNG's Comments on the First Draft

Our shorthand on the Policy, tied to the sections they speak to. Copy a comment as your own view, or send the full letter to the Principal Secretary by email.

Human-Wildlife Conflict and Coexistence

Relevant policy sub section: 8.8 Ecological Connectivity and Biodiversity Conservation

Policy Sub Section 8.8 should more explicitly recognise human-wildlife conflict and the need to promote coexistence in landscapes shared by people and wildlife.

The situational analysis should address the impacts of human-wildlife interactions on community safety and livelihoods, alongside the importance of supporting coexistence, with corresponding policy commitments to support community led approaches.

Add new policy statement 8.8.6

The Government and county governments shall:

8.8.6 Support community-led coexistence approaches that integrate local and Indigenous knowledge, community participation and sustainable livelihood opportunities into wildlife conservation and landscape management.

Public Participation

Relevant section: 11.7 Public Participation in Environmental Governance

Policy Sub Section 11.7 should address the gap between the constitutional requirement for meaningful public participation and its implementation in environmental decision-making.

Recent concerns surrounding developments in areas of significant ecological importance highlight the need for greater transparency, accountability and safeguards to ensure that participation is meaningful.

Processes should enable the public to access relevant information, express their views freely and influence decisions, with clear documentation of how public submissions are considered in final outcomes.

Strengthen existing policy statement 11.7.1

The Government shall:

11.7.1 Set and enforce national standards for public participation in environmental decisions that are prior, meaningful, informed, transparent and inclusive, with reasonable notice, translation and accessibility for persons with disabilities, and safeguards against intimidation, manipulation or undue influence to ensure that the public can express their views freely.

Youth in Environmental Justice

Relevant section: 11.6 Gender, Social Inclusion and Environmental Justice

While Policy Sub Section 11.6 recognises youth in environmental justice, participation and inclusion, it does not adequately address barriers such as limited funding, technical capacity, mentorship, professional experience and access to decision making.

The Policy should establish practical mechanisms to support youth led environmental action through accessible financing, skills development, mentorship and meaningful involvement in policy development, implementation and monitoring, with measurable indicators to track progress.

Add new policy statement 11.6.7, after 11.6.6

The Government and county governments shall:

11.6.7 Establish adequately resourced mechanisms to strengthen youth participation and leadership in environmental governance at national and county levels, including accessible financing, technical capacity, mentorship, professional development and decision-making opportunities, with measurable indicators to monitor progress.

Energy Transition, Environmental Integrity and Equitable Benefit-Sharing

Relevant policy sub sections: 10.6 Energy Transition, Energy Security and Environmental Integrity; 10.9 Just Transition, Decent Work and Equitable Benefit-Sharing

Energy development presents environmental and social challenges, including cumulative impacts on biodiversity, water resources and sensitive ecosystems, as well as risks associated with land acquisition, displacement and the unequal distribution of project benefits.

The Policy should strengthen environmental safeguards and establish clear commitments to meaningful community participation, equitable benefit sharing and protection of affected communities' rights and livelihoods.

Add new policy statements

The Government shall:

10.6.7 Align energy development with Kenya's climate commitments and strengthen environmental safeguards, including cumulative impact assessments, to protect biodiversity, water resources and sensitive ecosystems.

10.9.5 Strengthen community participation and equitable benefit sharing in large scale land based energy projects through enforceable Community Development Agreements and adequate safeguards for land acquisition, compensation and resettlement.

The Letter

To the Principal Secretary

The Principal Secretary

State Department for Environment and Climate Change

Ministry of Environment, Climate Change and Forestry

Nairobi, Kenya

RE: SUBMISSION OF COMMENTS ON THE NATIONAL ENVIRONMENT POLICY, 2026 (FIRST DRAFT)

Dear Principal Secretary,

Thank you for the opportunity to submit comments on the First Draft of the National Environment Policy, 2026.

We welcome the revision of the Policy as an important step towards strengthening Kenya's environmental governance framework in line with constitutional obligations, evolving environmental jurisprudence, devolution and emerging national and international environmental commitments. The draft provides an important basis for the protection, conservation and sustainable management of Kenya's environment and natural resources for present and future generations.

We respectfully recommend strengthening specific provisions to ensure that the Policy translates its objectives and guiding principles into practical, inclusive and accountable measures. In particular, we propose amendments relating to human-wildlife coexistence, meaningful public participation, youth inclusion in environmental governance, and environmental safeguards and equitable benefit-sharing in the energy transition.

Specifically, we recommend that:

Human-wildlife conflict and coexistence: Section 8.8 be strengthened to recognise the impacts of human-wildlife interactions on community safety and livelihoods, and include a policy commitment supporting community-led coexistence approaches that integrate local and Indigenous knowledge, community participation and sustainable livelihood opportunities.

Public participation: Policy statement 11.7.1 be strengthened by requiring enforceable national standards for prior, meaningful, informed, transparent and inclusive participation in environmental decision-making, including safeguards against intimidation, manipulation and undue influence, and measures to ensure accessibility and the free expression of public views.

Youth in environmental justice: A new policy statement, 11.6.7, be introduced to establish adequately resourced mechanisms for youth participation and leadership in environmental governance, including accessible financing, technical capacity, mentorship, professional development and meaningful decision-making opportunities, supported by measurable indicators.

Energy transition and equitable benefit-sharing: A new policy statement 10.6.7 to align energy development with Kenya's climate commitments and require stronger environmental safeguards, including cumulative impact assessments. New Policy statement 10.9.5 should also strengthen community participation and equitable benefit-sharing through enforceable Community Development Agreements and adequate safeguards for land acquisition, compensation and resettlement in large-scale land-based energy projects.

These recommendations seek to strengthen the implementation of the Policy's existing objectives and principles by addressing identified gaps in environmental protection, community participation, social inclusion and accountability. We believe their incorporation would contribute to a more effective and equitable policy framework that safeguards biodiversity and ecosystems while recognising the rights, livelihoods and contributions of communities and young people.

We appreciate the opportunity to contribute to the review process and respectfully request that these recommendations be considered in the development of the next draft of the National Environment Policy, 2026. We remain supportive of efforts to strengthen environmental governance and advance the sustainable management of Kenya's natural resources.

Yours sincerely,
